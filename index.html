General Principles

Each item is examined individually. Record its characteristics in the applet, then set it aside and proceed to the next item. This approach lets you capture multiple attributes per item without large-scale physical sorting.

Categories cannot anticipate every type of trash found on the highway. Use the Notes field for anything unusual. Large debris that won't fit in bags may be documented separately with descriptive notes.

When in doubt, record what you can observe and add a note. An imperfect entry is better than a skipped item.
Counting & Unit Rules

These rules keep item counts consistent between sorters and keep the plastic weight accurate.

Whole cups count as one unit. If a cup arrives intact with its lid and/or straw still attached, record the cup, lid, and straw together as a single item. Classify that item by the material of the cup — a paper cup with a plastic lid and straw is one Paper/Cardboard item, not three items.

But separate them before weighing. After recording the unit, physically detach the lid and straw and set them with the plastics. They count once toward the cup's material for the item count, and their mass is captured in the bag's plastic weight. The count and the weight are answering two different questions, so they are handled differently on purpose.

An intact McDonald's cup with lid and straw → one item, material: Paper/Cardboard, Food/Beverage Container + Takeout Container, brand: McDonald's. Then pull the lid and straw off and put them in the plastics pile before weighing.

Loose lids, straws, and caps are their own items. If you find a lid, straw, bottle cap, or pull tab on its own with no matching container, record it as its own item under Food/Beverage Container → Other Food Packaging, with its own material.

Measurements use the longest side. Every item measurement in this study is the length of the item's longest side, measured as found.

Quantity & Presets

Roadside litter contains large numbers of identical small items — cigarette butts, paper fragments, wood pieces. Recording those one at a time is slow and adds nothing. The Quantity field lets one record stand for a whole pile.

How to use it. Sort the pile out of the bag, count it, fill in the form once, set the quantity, and save. A record of 47 cigarette butts is one entry with quantity 47 — not 47 entries.

Split the pile whenever a field would differ. Quantity applies to items that are alike in every recorded respect. If the butts are a mix of two brands, that is two records. If some paper fragments are large and some small, that is two records. When in doubt, split — a slightly finer breakdown costs little and cannot be recovered later if lumped together.

Size means typical size for the group. With a quantity above 1, record the size that best represents the pile rather than measuring each piece. If the sizes vary enough that one label misleads, split the record.

Brand applies to the whole group. Only give a brand when every item in the group carries it.

38 identical unbranded cigarette butts → one record: material Other, Smoking Paraphernalia, quantity 38. Then 6 Marlboro butts → a second record with brand Marlboro, quantity 6.

Presets. Once you have filled in a combination you will use repeatedly, tap Save current form as a preset and name it. Afterwards one tap restores the whole form and you only set the quantity. Presets are stored on the device, not in the sheet, so each volunteer builds their own — and a shared device keeps them between sessions.

Save & Repeat. Saves the record but leaves the form exactly as it is, for when the next group differs in only one field. Change that field and save again.

The item counter in the banner shows total items, not number of records, so a bag with one 47-butt record reads 47.

Material Categories

Material categories are mutually exclusive. Assign the primary material by volume. The first four categories (Paper/Cardboard, Plastic, Glass, Metal) correspond roughly to the 2022 statewide study and enable direct comparison.

  • Paper/Cardboard: Paper packaging, cardboard, napkins, paper cups, receipts. A paper cup with a thin plastic lining is still Paper/Cardboard — the plastic lining is not the primary material by volume.
  • Plastic: Bottles, containers, wrappers, bags, foam products. Foam is Plastic.
  • Glass: Bottles, fragments, and other glass materials. Fragments are Glass.
  • Metal: Aluminum cans, metal containers, other metallic debris.
  • Wood: Wood scraps, lumber pieces, pallet fragments.
  • Textile: Fabrics and clothing, including technical exercise gear made primarily of synthetic plastic fibers — classify by the item's function (clothing/textile), not by the polymer content.
  • Other: Genuinely mixed-material or unidentifiable items that do not clearly fit another category. Use sparingly.
A mylar balloon: the primary material is plastic film → Plastic. A paper bag with staples → Paper/Cardboard. A tire fragment → Other (rubber), or use the Automotive Debris functional category to flag it.
Functional Categories

Functional categories are overlapping — an item can belong to multiple categories simultaneously. Select all that apply.

  • Food/Beverage Container: Anything that held, wrapped, or served food or drink. Use sub-options to specify further.
    • Takeout Container: Any container from a takeout or fast-food meal. Utensils belong here — forks, knives, sporks, chopsticks, and stir sticks are all recorded as Takeout Container.
    • Beverage Container: Any container for a beverage.
      • Alcoholic: Beer cans, liquor bottles, wine bottles, hard seltzer cans, and other alcohol packaging.
      • Redeemable (Bottle Bill): Cans and bottles that carry an Oregon deposit. See the Redeemable section below for the exact test.
    • Other Food Packaging: The catch-all for food-related items that are not a takeout or beverage container — food wrappers, bottle caps, cup lids, pull tabs, and straws.
  • Commercial Paperwork & Tags: Paper and tag waste originating from commercial transport and freight. A key category for hauler accountability.
    • Lumber Tags: Identification tags that detach from lumber loads and blow onto the road, even from properly secured loads.
    • Paperwork: Bills of lading, manifests, invoices, delivery slips, packing lists, inspection forms.
    • Other Tags & Labels: Shipping labels, barcode stickers, pallet placards, and any commercial tag that is not a lumber tag.
  • Fasteners, Packaging & Wrappers: Non-food wrapping, containment, and fastening material — plastic banding and strapping, shrink and pallet wrap, bubble wrap, zip ties, twine, tape, cardboard box fragments, foam packing blocks.
    • Commercial: Clearly from freight or industrial packaging — heavy banding, pallet wrap, bulk strapping.
    • Non-commercial: Consumer-scale packaging — a torn Amazon mailer, retail blister packaging, a bread tie.
    • Unknown: Cannot tell. Use freely.
  • Advertising & Promotion: Flyers, door hangers, brochures, coupon mailers, yard-sign fragments, promotional handouts, business cards, campaign material.
  • Toxic Fluid Container: Antifreeze, motor oil, weedkiller, and similar containers. Even empty containers count.
  • Automotive Debris: Vehicle parts, tire fragments, bumper pieces, and related materials.
  • Construction/Demolition Debris: Wood scraps, drywall, insulation, nails, concrete, cement.
  • Lawn/Garden Care: Lawn tools, fertilizer bags, pesticide containers, mulch packaging.
  • Marina-Related: Anything originating from or associated with the Washougal marina — rope, netting, buoys, boat parts.
  • Smoking Paraphernalia: Cigarette butts, vape waste, vape cartridges, lighters, cigarette packaging.
  • Attire: Clothing, gloves, shoes, hats, PPE items.
  • Furniture: Chairs, cushions, furniture fragments.
  • Other: Items that do not fit any category above. Add a note describing what it is so the category list can be improved.

Food wrappers vs. packaging — the deciding rule: if it held or wrapped food or drink, it goes under Food/Beverage Container → Other Food Packaging. If it wrapped, bundled, or fastened anything else, it goes under Fasteners, Packaging & Wrappers. A candy bar wrapper is food packaging. The shrink wrap around a case of candy bars is Fasteners/Packaging.

A beer can → Food/Beverage Container + Beverage Container + Alcoholic + Redeemable. A vape pen → Smoking Paraphernalia. A lumber tag → Commercial Paperwork & Tags + Lumber Tags. A strip of plastic banding off a freight pallet → Fasteners, Packaging & Wrappers + Commercial. A crushed bottle cap → Food/Beverage Container + Other Food Packaging.
Redeemable — Bottle Bill Definition

"Redeemable" is not a judgment about whether something looks recyclable or carries a recycling symbol. It means the container carries a deposit under the Oregon Bottle Bill. Use this legal definition, not your own assessment.

Only cans and bottles qualify. Cartons, pouches, boxes, and cups are never marked Redeemable in this study, even if the beverage inside would otherwise be covered.

The test has three parts — all must be true:

  • Form: a can or bottle made of metal, glass, or plastic.
  • Size: between 4 ounces and 1.5 liters.
  • Contents: a drinkable beverage that is not on the excluded list.

Excluded beverages: distilled spirits; wine, unless it is in a can; infant formula; liquid meal replacements; and anything listing dairy milk or a plant-based milk as the first ingredient.

Everything else pourable is generally covered — water, soda and other carbonated drinks, beer and malt beverages, hard seltzer, kombucha, juice, tea, coffee drinks, sports drinks, and energy drinks.

A 12 oz aluminum energy drink can → Redeemable. A 2-liter soda bottle → too large, not Redeemable. A wine bottle → excluded (wine only counts in a can). A milk jug → excluded. A juice box → not a can or bottle, so not Redeemable. A 16 oz plastic water bottle → Redeemable.

When you cannot determine the size or contents of a badly degraded container, leave Redeemable unchecked and add a note.

Likely Source

Likely source is a judgment call made by the sorter. This field is optional, and Unknown is a fully correct and acceptable answer — do not feel pressured to guess.

  • Intentional Litter: Items believed to have been deliberately discarded from a vehicle or by a pedestrian. Fast-food packaging, cigarette butts, and single-use drink containers are common examples.
  • Unsecured Loads: Materials believed to have fallen or blown from vehicle loads that were not properly secured. Construction debris, loose household items.
  • Commercial Haulers: Items that blew off secured commercial loads. Lumber tags are the archetypal example — they detach and blow off even from loads that appear properly secured. Plastic banding, packing film, and cardboard from pallets are other examples.
  • Unknown: Cannot determine the source with any reasonable confidence. Use freely — it is better to record Unknown than to record a guess as a fact.
"Unknown" is a data point, not a failure. The statewide study assigned likely source at the category level; we assign it per item, which means more unknowns are expected and appropriate.
Brand Information

Brand data helps assess corporate responsibility for highway pollution and is a key goal of this study. Record any identifiable brand — search the dropdown or type a brand name.

Beverages are recorded by type, not by brand. For water, soda, energy drinks, sports drinks, beer, and other beverages, select the generic "(all brands)" entry rather than the specific label. These categories are tracked at the beverage-type level, so a Coke can and a Pepsi can are both recorded as Soda (all brands).

  • Water (all brands) — bottled still or sparkling water
  • Soda (all brands) — carbonated soft drinks
  • Energy drink (all brands)
  • Sports drink (all brands) — electrolyte and hydration drinks
  • Other non-alcoholic beverage (all brands) — juice, tea, coffee drinks, kombucha, milk
  • Beer (all brands) — beer and malt beverages
  • Other alcoholic beverage (all brands) — wine, liquor, cider, and unnamed ready-to-drink cocktails

Named brands are still recorded for food and retail outlets, tobacco and vape products, and specific ready-to-drink alcohol brands the study tracks individually (Buzz Balls, Sipzz, White Claw, Truly).

Add new brands as you encounter them by typing directly into the brand field. Brand attribution is ad-hoc and cumulative: ECCA will have complete freedom to document any brand encountered.

A crushed Modelo can → brand: Beer (all brands). A Buzz Balls container → brand: Buzz Balls. A faded fast-food cup where you can still read "McDonald's" → brand: McDonald's. A generic plastic bag with no markings → leave brand blank.

Only record a brand when you can confidently identify it. Degraded or ambiguous branding should be left blank rather than guessed.

Size & Fragmentation

Size is measured along the item's longest side, as found. This applies to every item measurement in the study. Measure it as you find it — do not estimate what it would have been before it was broken or degraded.

For an irregular or crumpled item, measure the greatest straight-line distance between any two points on the item in its current state. Do not flatten or unfold it first.

  • Small: Less than 3 inches (roughly a cigarette butt, a bottle cap)
  • Medium: 3–6 inches (a standard soda can, a candy wrapper)
  • Large: 6 inches – 2 feet (a plastic bottle, a newspaper, a paper bag)
  • Very Large: Greater than 2 feet (a tire, a large piece of cardboard, furniture)

Fragment: Mark this if the item is clearly a piece of a larger broken item — scattered plastic shards, broken glass, torn sections of a bag. The fragment itself is measured as found, not reconstructed.

A plastic bottle cap: Small. Half of a glass bottle: Medium or Large depending on actual size — measure the fragment, not the original bottle. A tire fragment: depends on actual size of fragment found.
Workflow Overview

The applet is organized around a three-stage workflow for each bag:

  • 1. Bag tab: Enter transect ID, bag number, bag weight, and optionally your name and field notes. Save the bag record — this creates an active bag and takes you to the Items tab.
  • 2. Items tab: Enter items one group at a time — use Quantity for piles of identical items rather than repeating the form. Press Save & Next Item to record it and immediately get a blank form for the next item — this is the button you'll use for almost every item. When you reach the last item in the bag, press Save & Finish Bag instead, which records it and takes you straight to the Plastic Weight tab. The banner at the top shows a running count of items recorded for the current bag.
  • 3. Plastic Weight tab: After all items in the bag are entered, record the plastic weight and press Confirm Bag. This marks all items as confirmed and returns you to the Bag tab for the next bag.

You cannot access the Items or Plastic Weight tabs without an active bag. The Review tab lets you return to any previous bag to edit items or re-confirm weights, and includes a Local Backup section for exporting this device's records as CSV if the sheet is unreachable.

If you realize mid-way through a bag that you entered the wrong bag number, go to the Bag tab and change the bag number — the app will offer to update all items already recorded for that bag.
